5. OBSERVATIONS FROM iTRANSFORMER RESULTS
- Stable phase: MAE 26.74 and RMSE 32.51 establish a baseline before the imposed drift period.
- Drift phase: MAE 59.96 and RMSE 72.12; MAE is approximately 124% higher than stable-phase MAE (59.9613 / 26.7431 - 1).
- Recovery phase: MAE declines to 33.60 and RMSE to 39.24; recovery MAE remains approximately 26% higher than stable-phase MAE.
- Overall evaluation: MAE 40.16, RMSE 51.07 across 595 held-out targets.
- The period-specific error changes are consistent with strong sensitivity to the introduced drift regime. They do not alone establish the precise causal mechanism.
- Do not claim model superiority or statistical significance without consistent baselines and additional analysis.

6. OBSERVATIONS FROM ERROR-ANALYSIS GRAPH (VISUAL)
- The chart shows absolute error per observation and a 30-observation rolling mean absolute error.
- Red dashed line at index 600 marks drift onset; green dashed line at index 800 marks recovery onset.
- During the stable phase, the rolling error generally lies around 20–35, with intermittent spikes.
- Immediately after index 600, errors become larger and more volatile; individual absolute errors often exceed 100 and some approach or exceed 150.
- During the drift phase, the rolling error rises to roughly 60–75 early in the phase and remains elevated with fluctuations.
- After index 800, the rolling error declines toward roughly 30–40, though intermittent spikes remain.
- Rolling averages straddle phase boundaries; the rolling curve should not be interpreted as an instantaneous response to a regime change.
- The graph visually supports the numeric period-wise metrics and motivates testing adaptive updating.

7. INTERPRETATION AND PAPER CONTRIBUTION (PROVISIONAL)
- Comparing static and adaptive forecasting models under known stable, drift, and recovery periods may provide a useful empirical contribution.
- The drift-period performance drop motivates a controlled test of drift-aware adaptation, but improvement has not yet been demonstrated for iTransformer + ADWIN.
- Potential paper angles: robustness to drift, adaptation latency, recovery behavior, computational cost, and fair comparison of classical/recurrent/transformer methods.
- A combination of existing components alone is not sufficient evidence of novelty. The paper needs rigorous evaluation, transparent methodology, and a clearly articulated contribution.
